# Lossless JPEG XL encode memory, one-shot and chunked, 2026-11-15

Issue [#223](https://github.com/sebastian-software/ferrisma/issues/223).
**Memory, not timing**: peak resident memory of a fresh process is valid on a
shared machine; no timing is reported here. Evidence and tools:
[evidence-2026-11-15-jxl-lossless-memory](evidence-2026-11-15-jxl-lossless-memory/README.md).

## Question

The engine's lossless JPEG XL output used the port's fixed one-shot entry
(`LosslessThunderConfig`, buffering 0), measured on 2026-11-12 at 70-76 B/px
against 79 B/px for C++ `cjxl --buffering=0` and 27 B/px for `cjxl`'s
default, chunked mode. The port's public encoder now encodes the same
lossless effort-2 profile on the original's chunked path (libjxl-rs
`PORT_STATUS.md`, block "encoder lossless streaming"). What does each path
need, port and original, at 12 and 24 MP and below one DC group?

## Method

- Host: Apple Silicon (10 cores, 32 GB), macOS, shared with other agents;
  every run under the shared gate lock, one fresh process per run, two runs
  per case, the larger value reported.
- Tool: `/usr/bin/time -l`, "maximum resident set size" (peak RSS, the
  method of the 2026-11-12 analysis). The port's probe also counts its live
  heap bytes in a counting global allocator (heap peak).
- Port: `lossless_memory_probe` (release, built from the repository root as
  the engine builds): `fixed` is `LosslessThunderConfig::encode_pixels`;
  `api0` and `api-default` are the public encoder (JxlEncoderSetFrameLossless,
  effort 2, `uses_original_profile`, `JxlColorEncodingSetToSRGB`,
  JxlEncoderAddImageFrame, ProcessOutput into a growing vector) with
  buffering 0 and with the default buffering. The process holds the input
  once (3 B/px RGB, 4 B/px RGBA).
- Original: pinned `cjxl v0.13.0 a16d32d [_SCALAR_]`, `-d 0 -e 2
  --num_threads=0 -x color_space=RGB_D65_SRG_Rel_SRG`, with `--buffering=0`
  and with its default.
- Inputs: the 24 MP photograph of 2026-11-12 (`photo24`, 6000x4000), the
  12 MP CI photograph (`photo12`, 4032x3024), top-left crops of `photo24`
  (512x1024: 8 groups, the largest one-shot frame at the default buffering;
  700x700: 9 groups; 1024x1024; 2048x2048: one full DC group; 3000x2000),
  each opaque RGB and RGBA.

Bytes: for every input the port's `api-default` file equals `cjxl`'s default
file and `fixed`/`api0` equal `cjxl --buffering=0` (SHA-256 in the results);
at 512x1024 all five are the same file (the original does not stream at 8
groups). The 24 MP one-shot file equals the 2026-11-12 `mountain-lossless.jxl`.

## Reading

- **Chunked against one-shot, 24 MP:** the port needs 19.9 B/px (RGB) and
  28.9 B/px (RGBA) on the chunked path against 74.1 and 96.9 B/px one-shot
  through the same API; the original 26.5 and 34.7 against 77.9 and 100.5.
  At 12 MP: 33.0 / 42.3 against 75.3 / 96.0 (original 37.4 / 48.0). The port
  stays within the original's figure on every chunked case.
- **The cost is not per pixel below one DC group.** The chunked encode works
  on one 2048x2048 DC group at a time, so up to about 4 MP it costs about as
  much per pixel as a DC group does: 52.3 B/px RGB (70.0 RGBA) at 2048x2048,
  62-66 B/px at 0.5-1 MP, where a constant dominates. Above it, RSS grows by
  roughly 6-15 B/px for each further pixel (the frame copy, the kept section
  writers and the output); the heap peak grows by about 6 B/px. RSS keeps
  memory the allocator already released (the heap peak of 3000x2000 is
  212 MB against 309 MB RSS), so the reservation is fitted to RSS.
- **One-shot at the small end:** 85.8 B/px RGB and 113.2 B/px RGBA at
  512x1024. The 2026-11-13 constant of 76 B/px (RGB) did not cover this
  size; the alpha factor was derived (4/3), here it is measured.

## Reservation model (engine, `src/jxl_sink.rs`)

The engine reserves its RGBA8 frame (4 B/px) plus, for the codec, the model
below minus the input the measured process held (3 or 4 B/px), so that frame
plus reservation are at least every measured peak above (checked by
`jxl_output_tests::input_frame_and_measured_lossless_peaks_are_reserved`):

| Term | Opaque RGB | RGBA |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| one-shot, at most 8 groups: per pixel | 76 | 97 |
| chunked, more than 8 groups: per pixel of the frame | 16 | 20 |
| chunked: per pixel of `min(frame, 2048x2048)` | 53 | 70 |
| constant, both paths | 12 MiB | 12 MiB |

The terms are an upper envelope of the measured maxima: engine frame plus
reservation exceed every measured peak by at least 6 % (RGBA at 6 and 12 MP;
9-45 % at the other chunked points, 7 % and 18 % at the 512x1024 one-shot
size). They are not a bound for other content or hosts. Engine frame plus reservation at
24 MP opaque: 643 MB (26.8 B/px) instead of 1,824 MB (76 B/px).

Under the default 256 MiB budget (owner decision 2026-11-15: unchanged) the
largest opaque lossless output is 3,655,036 pixels (70 B/px plus 12 MiB below
one DC group), against about 3.53 MP before (76 B/px); with alpha 2,842,806
pixels (90 B/px plus 12 MiB). The chunked path helps large images: a 24 MP
opaque image needs a budget of about 614 MiB instead of about 1,740 MiB.

## Limits of this evidence

One host (macOS AArch64), two photographs and their crops, two runs each;
x86-64 and Linux allocators may retain memory differently. Gray and 16-bit
input are not measured; the engine writes only 8-bit RGB and RGBA. The lossy
estimate (`estimated_codec_bytes`) is not measured here (#223, third box).
