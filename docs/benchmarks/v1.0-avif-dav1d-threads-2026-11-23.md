# AVIF: the dav1d decode on several threads, 2026-11-23

Since 2026-11-22 the dav1d port is the engine's default AVIF decoder
(ADR-0031, amendment of that day). Until this record the reader
(`ferrisma-avif-read`) kept the AV1 decode on one thread for both decoders;
only libavif's YUV-to-RGB conversion used the engine's threads. The reason
was libaom's: its row-based multithreading holds coefficient buffers for
the whole frame. The owner approved measuring dav1d's decode threads and
enabling them for dav1d if they pay off. This record is that measurement;
it is the decision basis for `v1/avif-dav1d-threads`.

- **Source:** `main` at `d886949e`. `new` is the branch's reader as
  committed in `4d15574e` (`Decoder::set_threads` sets libavif's
  `maxThreads` for dav1d), `main` the same example built with `main`'s
  reader source. Both are `crates/ferrisma-avif-read/examples/decode_still.rs`
  (the engine's reader, nearest upsampling to RGB8), so they time exactly
  what the engine runs.
- **Evidence:** [evidence-2026-11-23-avif-dav1d-threads](evidence-2026-11-23-avif-dav1d-threads/):
  every `run_interleaved.py` record (JSON, every sample), the plane hashes,
  the scripts, the host files.

## What libavif asks of dav1d

libavif's dav1d glue (`codec_dav1d.rs`, `dav1dCodecGetNextImage`) opens
dav1d with `n_threads` = libavif's `maxThreads` (clamped to 1..256) and
`max_frame_delay` = 1. dav1d then runs one frame context (`n_fc` = 1) and
`n_tc` = `n_threads` worker threads: no frame threading, but the tile and
superblock-row tasks of the one frame (entropy decode, reconstruction,
loop filter, CDEF, loop restoration) are spread over the pool. libavif
copies `maxThreads` into the codec of every item, color and alpha and each
grid cell, before that codec decodes. libaom gets `cfg.threads` =
`maxThreads` with its default row multithreading.

## What the 2026-11-22 "all threads" columns parallelized

The `tN` columns of the [2026-11-22 record](v1.0-decoders-2026-11-22.md)
come from `codecs/libavif-rs/examples/decode_codec.rs`, which sets
`decoder.maxThreads` = N and leaves `avifRGBImage.maxThreads` at libavif's
default of one. So `port-dav1d:tN` there is exactly the dav1d decode on N
threads with a serial YUV-to-RGB conversion, not what the engine ran: the
engine's reader (`port-aom-reader:t1`) was measured on one thread only,
and the engine at the time threaded only the conversion. Re-read from that
record's hosted JSON (4 vCPUs,
[table](evidence-2026-11-23-avif-dav1d-threads/hosted-2026-11-22-dav1d-threads.md)):
dav1d on four threads against one took 1/1.39–1/1.61 of the time on GH
(Intel) and 1/1.56–1/1.80 on BS (AMD) at 12 and 24 MP, 0.94–1.06× at web
sizes; its peak RSS grew by 3–10 %. libaom on four threads grew about
fourfold in RSS (12 MP: 251 → 998 MB) and got slower on GH.

The memory cost is dav1d's per-thread task contexts and stacks: a few MB
at web sizes, 6–20 MB at 12 and 24 MP. libaom's row multithreading, for
comparison, takes about four times its one-thread RSS (2026-11-22: 12 MP
251 → 998 MB) whatever the thread count, which is why it stays on one
decode thread. At one thread `new` and `main` time the same (the change
does nothing there).

Against the `main` reader at the same thread count (dav1d on one thread,
conversion on N threads): macOS, ten threads, 1.77–1.96× faster at 12 MP
and 2.19–2.77× at 24 MP, 0.92–1.12× at web sizes; Colima, four threads,
1.23–1.70× at 12 MP and 0.97–2.19× at 24 MP.

At web sizes the gain is small (macOS) or none (hosted x86-64: 0.94–1.06×,
under half a millisecond either way at 0.34 MP): a single-tile 0.34 MP
frame has few superblock rows to spread. The Colima web-size figures are
dominated by Rosetta translating each fresh process (a 0.34 MP decode
takes 100–300 ms there against 5–9 ms on hosted x86-64) and say nothing
about the decoder; only its 12 and 24 MP rows are indicative.

## Pixel identity

- **RGB8, every sample:** on both hosts every sample of every candidate
  (`new-dav1d` at each thread count, `main-dav1d`, `new-libaom`) wrote the
  same RGB8 as `new-dav1d:t1`, on all 16 inputs (macOS 16 × 8 candidates,
  Colima 16 × 7, nine repetitions and a warm-up each).
- **YUV planes and full-depth RGB:** for each input and each thread count
  (macOS 1, 2, 4, 10; Colima 1, 2, 4) the Y, U and V planes and the 8/16-bit
  RGB of dav1d equal those of dav1d on one thread, and the planes equal
  libaom's (macOS 80 decodes, Colima 64).
- **QEMU cross-check:** the same plane dump under `qemu-x86_64 -cpu
  Haswell-v4` (no Rosetta, AVX2 selected) on threads 1, 2 and 4: 48 of 48
  decodes identical to one thread, to the Rosetta run and to libaom's planes;
  the planes also equal the macOS ARM64 ones.
- **Committed corpus:** `dav1d_threads_decode_the_avif_corpus_identically`
  (81 of 95 files decode, YUV/alpha planes and RGB on 2, 3, 4 and 8 threads
  against one) passes on macOS ARM64 (debug and release), in Colima
  (release, SIGSEGV trap preloaded, no trap fired) and under `qemu-x86_64
  -cpu Haswell-v4` (release, 191 s).

## Noise and limits

- **macOS ARM64:** a shared machine; both gate slots were held during the
  pass, but other work ran: 1-minute load 8.6–14.8 on 10 cores. The
  one-thread medians are 5–8 % above the 2026-11-22 record's
  `port-dav1d:t1` (for example 12 MP 8-bit 4:2:0 166 against 156 ms), and
  that record's own `tN` (decode on ten threads, serial conversion) gave
  100 ms where this record's reader gives 67 ms with the conversion
  threaded too. The ten-thread figures are the most exposed to the load;
  under less load they would be the same or better. One pass only: a
  second pass waited for the gate slots, which another agent held.
- **x86-64 Colima:** 4 vCPUs under Rosetta on the same Mac, load 1.3–5.8
  inside the VM. Rosetta translates each fresh process, which dominates
  the web sizes and widens the spreads at 12 and 24 MP (for example 24 MP
  8-bit 4:4:4: 823–2198 ms at one thread). Indicative only; the hosted
  2026-11-22 rows are the real x86-64 timings of the dav1d decode on four
  threads (Intel and AMD, two runs each, spread 2.6–4.7 %).
- **Not measured:** multi-tile files (these inputs have one tile; dav1d
  also threads across tiles), alpha items, grids, image sequences, x86-64
  below AVX2, AVX-512 hosts, Linux ARM64 (no AVIF port build, ADR-0026),
  and the engine's CLI end to end. The engine passes the same thread count
  (`transform_threads`, resolved) to the reader, so the reader's timing is
  the decode stage's; `Engine::transcode_batch` gives each item one thread
  and is unchanged.

## Method

- **Inputs:** the 16 files of the 2026-11-22 record (photo12, photo24 and
  their 1.4 and 0.34 MP box-filtered versions; 8 and 10 bit, 4:2:0 and
  4:4:4; C `encode_still`, quality 60, speed 6, one thread, one tile),
  SHA-256 checked 16/16 against that record's `decoders-inputs.json`.
- **Candidates per input:** `new-dav1d` at every thread count, `main-dav1d`
  and `new-libaom` at one and all threads. One plan per input, a fresh
  process per sample, alternating order, one warm-up and nine repetitions
  ([run_interleaved.py](../../scripts/bench/ci-perf/run_interleaved.py)).
  The timer covers parse, AV1 decode and YUV-to-RGB8; peak RSS from
  `/usr/bin/time`.
- **Planes:** a scratch copy of `codecs/libavif-rs/examples/decode_planes.rs`
  (`scripts/zz_planes_threads.rs.txt`)
  with codec and thread arguments (in the evidence directory, not
  committed) writes the Y, U, V planes and the full-depth RGB per thread
  count; SHA-256 compared against one thread and against libaom.
- **Hosts:** local Apple M1 Pro (8+2 cores), macOS 27.2, the pinned
  nightly; x86-64 Linux in Colima (`dav1dx86`, 4 vCPUs, Rosetta, AVX2
  selected), the SIGSEGV trap preloaded; QEMU `qemu-x86_64 -cpu Haswell-v4`
  in the same container for the identity cross-check (Rosetta is not
  authoritative for AVX2 correctness).
