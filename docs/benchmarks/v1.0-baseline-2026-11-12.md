# v1.0 baseline measurements, 2026-11-12 (indicative; loaded host)

**Every number in this record is indicative and was taken on a loaded host.**
The Apple M1 Ultra used here was shared with two other agents that compiled
and ran tests with up to four jobs each. The 1-minute load average was between
about 3 and 20 during the runs and is stated per table. This run's own
untimed quality matching overlapped the section 1 and 3 timings. None of these numbers
is release-grade, a general performance claim, or a Sharp/libvips parity
claim. They give the v1.0 decisions (#53, #55, #93, #19, #68, #61) a first
interleaved A/B baseline at today's defaults, and each table says what an
idle-host re-run still has to confirm.

## Host, build and method

- **Host:** Apple M1 Ultra (Mac13,2), 20 cores, 64 GiB, macOS 27.0.1
  (26A434), Darwin 27.0.0 arm64.
- **Toolchain:** `rustc 1.100.0-nightly (5a2be9f5f 2026-09-06)`, LLVM 23.1.1,
  `cargo 1.100.0-nightly (3c0b53475 2026-09-04)`; release profile, no
  `RUSTFLAGS`.
- **Source:** commit `4f96bbc667f27bb8eb00c78d085211fd0eb4a12f`, empty tracked
  diff. Defaults at this commit: JPEG XL input through libjxl-rs, AVIF output
  through the workspace libavif/AOM writer.
- **Binaries** (SHA-256 in [environment.txt](evidence-2026-11-12-baseline/environment.txt)):
  `cargo build --locked --release -p ferrisma --features cli --bin ferrisma`;
  `cargo build --locked --release -p ferrisma-jxl-decoder --example jxl_decode_compare`
  (libjxl-rs at the workspace pin and jxl-oxide 0.12.6, default features off);
  the `ferrisma-libavif` adapter and the independent `ferrisma-quality-match`
  scorer (SSIMULACRA2 0.5.1, `avif-decode` 3.0.0) as
  [encoder-adoption](encoder-adoption.md) builds them. Lossless JPEG XL inputs
  came from a separate `--features cli,jpeg-xl-write` build in its own target
  directory, used only to create inputs.
- **Runner:** [run_interleaved.py](evidence-2026-11-12-baseline/run_interleaved.py).
  Every sample is a fresh process under `/usr/bin/time -l`. One warm-up round,
  then 7 repetitions; within each repetition every candidate runs once and
  the candidate order alternates (forward, reversed). Wall time includes
  process start-up and file I/O. "Internal" is the program's own timer
  (`elapsed_ms` of the decode harness or the CLI `report:` `total_us`). Max RSS
  is the whole process, not a codec allocation measure. Tables give the
  median and the min–max over the 7 measured samples. Load averages are the
  1-minute values before and after each sample. Raw samples, exact commands,
  output sizes and SHA-256 values are in the JSON files of
  [evidence-2026-11-12-baseline](evidence-2026-11-12-baseline/).
- Single-thread comparisons are the primary result: they suffer least from
  contention. All-core numbers are marked and are the least reliable.

## 1. JPEG XL decode: libjxl-rs versus jxl-oxide (#55, #93)

Harness: `jxl_decode_compare <input> <own|oxide> <full|crop> 1`, one decode
per process, native F32 samples, upright rendering, one decoder thread for
both (neither backend uses threads here: libjxl-rs runs pool tasks serially,
#100, and jxl-oxide is built without its rayon feature). The center crop is
the middle half of each dimension; `own` reconstructs the full frame and
slices it, `oxide` requests the region. The harness's `compare` mode checked
every input first: maximum absolute sample difference 2.98e-7 (flower VarDCT)
and 6.0e-8 (the three lossless inputs).

Inputs:

- **flower VarDCT**, 2268×1512, 455,570 bytes, SHA-256 `f202533a…6d4c14`:
  the JPEG-reconstruction JXL of the [2026-11-11 record](M2-jxl-input-switch.md),
  made by the pinned C++ libjxl oracle and retained locally with the hash that
  record states. It is the only photo-sized lossy (VarDCT) JPEG XL input
  available on this host: no `cjxl`, no ImageMagick JXL delegate, and the
  workspace encoder admits only lossless effort 2 (ADR-0039).
- **Lossless (modular) photos** written by the workspace encoder
  (`ferrisma <in> <out>.jxl W H …` from the `jpeg-xl-write` build, lossless
  effort 2, 8-bit RGB): flower 2268×1512 (3,207,665 bytes, `518da2c2…`), the
  dog photo upright 3024×4032 = 12.2 MP (9,729,084 bytes, `fa803e4c…`), and the
  mountain photo 6000×4000 = 24 MP (25,574,493 bytes, `69e7a926…`). Lossless
  modular decoding is a different workload from VarDCT; these rows do not
  describe lossy JPEG XL photos.

Load average 19.9–20.5 for the VarDCT row, 10.2–11.2 for the lossless rows.

Reading: the VarDCT gap of the 2026-11-11 record reproduces (then 3.6× full,
9.5× crop, 3 decodes per process on an idle host). On lossless photos the
workspace decoder is about 17–19 % faster for a full frame but uses about
1.8× the process RSS, and every crop costs a full decode (2.2–2.6× slower
than jxl-oxide's region decode). The end-to-end CLI rows in section 3 show
the same pattern through the engine.

**#93 parity reference (C++ libjxl): blocked.** The port's oracle
(`codecs/libjxl-rs/tools/oracle`, `HWY_COMPILE_ONLY_SCALAR`) cannot be built
in this worktree: the `upstream/libjxl` submodule and its `third_party`
(highway, brotli, skcms) are not initialized, and fetching them needs network
access this run did not use. The oracle also exposes no whole-image decode
request suitable for timing (its protocol is hex over stdin). Prebuilt oracle
binaries of other worktrees exist on this host, but their hashes differ from
the one the 2026-11-11 record used, so they were not used. An idle-host #93
measurement needs a pinned C++ `djxl` (scalar and SIMD builds) timed on the
same inputs.

**Idle-host re-run must confirm:** the VarDCT ratio at 12–48 MP (needs a lossy
JXL encoder or retained lossy photos), the lossless full-decode advantage,
and RSS; plus x86-64, and the C++ reference above.

## 2. AVIF encode: rav1e speed 6 versus workspace AOM speed 6/7 (#53)

Method: the existing encoder procedure ([encoder-adoption](encoder-adoption.md))
ran `prepare` and `match` at this commit for rav1e anchors 70, 80 and 90 (as
in the [non-timing record](M2-avif-encoder-default-evidence-2026-11-12.md)),
one thread and all cores, 10-bit 4:4:4, AOM speeds 6 and 7, on the four
default images plus two large originals: `elcito` (7952×5304, 42.2 MP,
independent photo, `--assume-srgb`) and `beach` (9504×6336, 60.2 MP, the whole
original, `--use-rendered-icc`). Each large input resizes to 1600×1067, so its
encode is photo-sized while decode and resize cover the full input. The
`mountain` and `deer` 24 MP lanes cannot run at this commit (EXIF defect in
section 3). Sharp 0.35.5, preinstalled at
`/private/tmp/ferramenta300-registry-consumer/node_modules` (the installation
the [2026-11-11 smoke](M2-corpus-smoke-2026-11-11.md) used), made the
reference images.

`encoder-check` exits 3, so the harness's own `encoder-time` phase refuses to
run. Rejected cells (nearest integer AOM quality more than 0.5 SSIMULACRA2
from the rav1e anchor) are the same six default-image cells as in the earlier
record (dog q70 t1, flower q80 t1, kodim03 q70 t0/t1, kodim23 q70 t0, kodim23
q80 t1), plus three elcito speed-6 cells (q70 t0 −1.117, q70 t1 −0.818, q80 t1
−0.524). All 12 beach cells passed. The match records are retained in
[matched-default.json](evidence-2026-11-12-baseline/matched-default.json),
[matched-elcito.json](evidence-2026-11-12-baseline/matched-elcito.json) and
[matched-beach.json](evidence-2026-11-12-baseline/matched-beach.json).
Timing therefore used the interleaved runner on **every** cell at its matched
nearest quality, with rejected cells kept and marked; draw no size or time
conclusion from a rejected cell. The AOM lane is the ordinary CLI default
(`--avif-encoder=libavif`); its output was byte-identical to the harness's
`ferrisma-libavif` match output (checked for flower q55 t1), and every timed
rav1e output was byte-identical to its match anchor. Every timed output was
identical across its 8 samples, and its independent rescore equalled its
match score.

Command per lane: `ferrisma <corpus>/<image>.jpg out.avif W H <profile>
--avif-encoder=<rav1e|libavif> --quality=Q --speed=S --encoder-threads=T
--transform-threads=T --chroma=444 [--budget-mb=2048]`, with T = 1 or 0 (all
cores). Wall time covers the whole process: decode, ICC, resize, encode, I/O.
Byte deltas compare real files at their own scores (within 0.5 of the anchor
unless rejected), not interpolated equal-score sizes.

Load average: 4.9–9.2 for the one-thread runs, 5.2–10.4 for the all-core runs
(per cell in [encoder-summary.json](evidence-2026-11-12-baseline/encoder-summary.json)).
Wall seconds are median (min–max) of 7; RSS is the maximum of the 7.

## 3. Decode stage times, RSS and orientation cost at 12 and 24 MP (#19, #68)

Release CLI, `--budget-mb=2048 --encoder-threads=1`, WebP output resized to
1600 px on the long side (lossy WebP is the cheapest built-in encoder, so the
encode stage stays small and constant). Inputs, all derived from real photos
and listed with hashes in [inputs.sha256](evidence-2026-11-12-baseline/inputs.sha256):

- **12 MP:** the dog photo (4032×3024 stored, sRGB ICC) with its EXIF APP1
  replaced by a one-entry EXIF orientation 1, 2 or 6
  ([set_orientation.py](evidence-2026-11-12-baseline/set_orientation.py));
  entropy data and ICC unchanged. WebP (`magick -quality 85`), AVIF
  (`magick -quality 60 -define heic:speed=9`, libheif 1.23.7) and lossless
  JXL (section 1) from the same pixels. All use `--use-rendered-icc`.
- **24 MP:** the mountain photo (6000×4000, no ICC) with its EXIF APP1 removed
  (see the defect below), then orientation 1, 2 or 6 inserted as above; WebP,
  AVIF and lossless JXL as for 12 MP. JPEG, WebP and AVIF use
  `--assume-srgb`; the lossless JXL is enum-sRGB and needs
  `--use-rendered-icc`, so its transform stage includes the ICC cost in the
  next table.

## Problems observed (proposed follow-ups)

1. JPEG EXIF `Orientation` stored as LONG makes real photographs fail with
   `MalformedInput` (two of three verified corpus photos).
2. JPEG XL VarDCT decode through libjxl-rs: 3.2× (full) and 8.8× (crop) the
   jxl-oxide time; crops always decode the full frame; about 1.8× the process
   RSS on lossless photos.
3. Rendered sRGB ICC costs about 470 ms per 24 MP on one transform thread
   (transform stage 5.7×), relevant to one-thread-per-image batch work.
4. AOM speed 6 is up to 84 % slower than rav1e speed 6 on the elcito photo at
   q90, the only image where it was not faster.
5. AVIF decode keeps about 330 MB of opaque memory at 24 MP (434 MB RSS
   against 103 MB managed).
6. JPEG decode is single-threaded and dominates all-core wall time for large
   inputs (441 ms of about 0.6 s for 42.2 MP elcito).
7. The 24 MP orientation-6 gather costs +87 ms on one thread, three times the
   mirror; the mirror (o2) still materializes the full 72 MB buffer.

## Blocked or not measured

- **C++ libjxl parity reference (#93):** the oracle's submodules are not
  initialized and need network access; prebuilt oracles of other worktrees
  differ from the recorded hash (section 1).
- **Lossy JPEG XL at 12–48 MP:** no lossy JPEG XL encoder on this host; only
  the 3.4 MP VarDCT flower exists.
- **Standalone libvips CLI comparison (#61):** `vips`/`vipsthumbnail` are not
  installed; no system packages were installed.
- **`mountain` and `deer` lanes:** rejected by the EXIF LONG-orientation defect
  (section 3); only an EXIF-stripped mountain derivative was measured.
- **48 MP decode and orientation, x86-64, any idle-host run:** not attempted.
- **Harness `encoder-time` and `time` phases:** not used. The former refuses
  any matrix with a rejected cell; the latter does not interleave lanes. No
  script was changed.
