# Encoder memory against the engine's reservations, 2026-11-16

**Memory, not timing**: peak resident memory of fresh processes; no timing
is reported. Evidence and tools:
[evidence-2026-11-16-encoder-memory](evidence-2026-11-16-encoder-memory/README.md).

## Question

The engine reserves memory for an encode against the caller's budget before
it starts (ADR-0004), from a model in bytes per pixel. On 2026-11-15 the
deep-output work reported, as a side result, that 8-bit lossless RGBA at
12.2 MP peaked at 50.1 B/px against 45.1 B/px in the model
([record](v1.0-jxl-deep-memory-2026-11-15.md), last section). A
reservation below the real peak lets a caller's budget be exceeded without
an error (ADR-0004; ADR-0042 D3). Does the model cover the measured peak of
every JPEG XL request it admits, lossless and lossy, opaque and with alpha,
8-bit and deep, from 0.5 to 24 MP; and do the AVIF and WebP writers'
reservations cover theirs?

## Method

Apple M1 Pro (10 cores, 32 GB), macOS 27.2.1, shared with other agents; the
pinned `nightly-2026-09-07`, the workspace `release` profile, native
AArch64, `main` at `00e1e83a`. One probe process at a time; three accepted
runs per case, the largest maximum resident set size (RSS) taken, as in the
2026-11-15 evidence; the peak memory footprint (which also counts
compressed and swapped pages) is recorded beside it, and a run whose
footprint exceeds its RSS by more than 2 %, that started below 20 % free
memory or during which swap grew is rejected and repeated. The probe holds
what the sink holds (its frame of four samples per pixel, compacted to
three when opaque) and makes the sink's bridge call. Inputs: photograph
crops, a 16-bit photograph mirrored to size (8 and 16 bits), and seeded
uniform noise, the content that compresses worst; sizes 512x1024 (8 groups,
the largest one-shot frame), 700x700 (9 groups, streaming), 2048x2048 (one
DC group), 2304x2048 (just above), 3000x2000, 4032x3024 and 6000x4000.
Lossy at distances 0.05, 1.0 and 2.9 (the admitted range is 0.05 to below
3.0). The 2026-11-15 records enter at the engine's frame (see below).

The machine was overloaded for most of the session (load average up to
about 190, up to 12.4 of 13.3 GB swap in use). The first measurements, run
in parallel, are discarded (README, "Disturbed runs"); every case below was
measured serially under the acceptance rule, from 03:01 to 04:37, with the
load between about 9 and 190. Under memory pressure the kernel can also
reclaim freed, reusable pages without a footprint change, which lowers RSS
and which the rule cannot see; the 10 % margin of the model is meant to
absorb that and the run-to-run spread of the same encode (up to about 7 %;
the live heap of a case is identical in every run). The footprint of a
rejected run enters the fit as a sample too (a lower bound of its peak),
which can only raise the model.

## The reported 12.2 MP gap

- The 2026-11-15 tool, `encode_pnm`, held the input file and a converted
  copy of it through the encode (8 B/px at 8-bit RGBA) where the sink holds
  one 4 B/px frame. Alternating on the same file it peaked at 595 and
  597 MB against 546 and 518 MB for the sink-shaped probe
  (`pnm-check.txt`). Part of the reported 50.1 B/px was the tool.
- The gap is real nonetheless. Measured as the sink holds it, 8-bit RGBA at
  4032x3024 peaks at 46.8 and 46.3 B/px (photograph crop, weld) against 45.1 B/px
  modeled, and at 50.0 B/px for noise: under-covered by up to about
  10 % (3 % for the photographs). Opaque noise at 4032x3024 and 3000x2000 is also under-covered,
  and so are the one-shot noise frames.
- **Cause.** The live heap (the probe's counting allocator) is far below the
  peak there: 27.7 B/px of live heap at 4032x3024 RGBA against 46 to
  50 B/px RSS. The difference is memory the allocator keeps after the first
  DC group: the encoder works on one 2048x2048 DC group at a time, and a
  second DC group of another shape (1984x2048, 2048x976, ...) does not reuse
  the first group's freed buffers, so the resident peak grows by up to one
  more DC group's working set. Just above one DC group the effect is
  plain: 8-bit noise with alpha at 2304x2048 has a live heap of 277.9 MB
  against 273.7 MB at 2048x2048 (+4 MB), but an RSS of 374.9 MB against
  332.3 MB (+43 MB, about 80 B/px of the second group's 0.5 MP); the
  live heap of a case is deterministic, RSS is not. The 2026-11-15 model fitted a
  per-frame slope through 12 and 24 MP of one photograph and had no term
  for this; it also was not measured on content that compresses badly
  (noise: larger output, more tokens), which raises the per-frame slope.
- **Lossy** was never measured: the sink reserved the bridge's estimate
  `estimated_codec_bytes`, derived from the source. It counted one 256x256
  group's buffers where the encoder works on a whole DC group, and no
  tokens, histograms, output or allocator retention: 7.6 to 68 B/px
  against 26 to 166 B/px measured, **every** lossy cell under-covered, by
  56 to 92 %.

## The model (src/jxl_sink.rs, `PeakModel`)

Peak of frame plus codec, per mode, alpha and depth class:

- one-shot (at most 8 groups): `constant + one_shot * px`
- streaming: `constant + frame * px + dc_group * min(px, D)
  + retained * min(max(px - D, 0), D)`, D = 2048 * 2048

`frame` covers the buffers that live through the whole encode (this frame,
the encoder's copy, frame-wide DC-resolution and section data, the output);
`dc_group` the working set of the one DC group being encoded; `retained`
the resident memory the allocator keeps from the first group while the
second allocates its own. The codec reservation is the peak minus the
engine's frame. Fitted by `tools/fit.py` to 10 % above the largest peak on
record of every case (A: the steepest least-squares slope of one content
above two DC groups, of the peaks or the live heap; then B, R and the
one-shot term as the least values that keep the margin; each alpha or deep
term at least the opaque or 8-bit one):

| Mode | Alpha | Depth | constant | one_shot | frame | dc_group | retained |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| lossless | no | 8 | 12 MiB | 87 | 14 | 51 | 38 |
| lossless | no | 9-16 | 12 MiB | 111 | 24 | 58 | 56 |
| lossless | yes | 8 | 12 MiB | 119 | 16 | 69 | 67 |
| lossless | yes | 9-16 | 12 MiB | 135 | 34 | 71 | 69 |
| lossy | no | 8 | 20 MiB | 100 | 9 | 102 | 62 |
| lossy | no | 9-16 | 20 MiB | 103 | 16 | 103 | 63 |
| lossy | yes | 8 | 20 MiB | 143 | 11 | 114 | 70 |
| lossy | yes | 9-16 | 20 MiB | 143 | 25 | 114 | 70 |

## Limits of this evidence

One host (macOS AArch64, its allocator), an overloaded machine (see
Method), three accepted runs per case where the machine allowed. 9 to 15
bits are covered by the 16-bit model. Images above 24 MP are not measured;
the limits above extrapolate the per-frame slope. Not measured, or with
fewer than three accepted runs, when the measuring stopped because the
machine did not calm down:

- lossy 16-bit noise at distance 2.9: 4032x3024 with alpha (rejected runs
  only, their footprint used), 6000x4000 opaque and with alpha;
- lossy at distance 0.05 except 8-bit RGBA at 2048x2048 and 4032x3024
  (noise and the 16-bit photograph at 8 bits); the measured cells peak
  below distance 2.9 or 1.0 at the same size;
- fewer than three accepted runs: 8-bit noise lossy at 4032x3024 with
  alpha (1.0), 6000x4000 opaque (1.0, 2.9) and with alpha (2.9), 16-bit
  noise 700x700 with alpha (1.0);
- the photograph contents lossy below 3000x2000 and opaque; noise was the
  largest content in every case where both were measured;
- gray output, other hosts and allocators.
