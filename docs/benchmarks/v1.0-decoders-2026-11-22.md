# AVIF decoders: the libaom port against the dav1d port, 2026-11-22

The engine encodes AVIF with the translated libaom in every case. For
decoding, the workspace has two translations: libaom (`ferrisma-libaom`, the
decoder the engine selects, ADR-0031) and dav1d (`ferrisma-dav1d` with
`ferrisma-dav1d-kernels`). The dav1d kernels were rebuilt in Rust and rolled
out to `main` on 2026-11-20 (merge `8e86ae17`; libavif-rs ADR 0062). This
record measures both decoders at current `main` against each other and
against stock C libaom and stock C dav1d. It is evidence for the owner's
decision on keeping dav1d as a second decoder. It ranks nothing and carries
no recommendation.

- **Source:** `main` at `f06033cd`, plus the bench tooling commits
  `3d771061` and `842de93a` on `v1/bench-decoders-2026-11-22`. Neither commit
  changes product code; both touch `scripts/bench/ci-perf/` and
  `.github/workflows/bench-v1-perf.yml` only.
- **Runs:** macOS ARM64 local, two passes. x86-64 Linux hosted, two runs of
  workflow "v1.0 CI performance", dispatched with `only=decoders`:
  [45015543707](https://github.com/sebastian-software/ferrisma/actions/runs/45015543707)
  and [45016849091](https://github.com/sebastian-software/ferrisma/actions/runs/45016849091).
  All jobs succeeded, all at head `842de93a`.
- **Evidence:** [evidence-2026-11-22-decoders](evidence-2026-11-22-decoders/),
  holding every `run_interleaved.py` record (JSON, every sample),
  `decoders-checks.json`, `decoders-inputs.json`, `avif-build.json` and the
  host files. The workflow artifacts are unmodified. In the local records the
  worktree paths are replaced by `<repo>`.

What the numbers show:

- **dav1d port against libaom port, one thread:** the dav1d port takes 0.50–0.60
  of the libaom port's time on x86-64 (both hosts, both runs) and 0.57–0.69
  on macOS ARM64. The ratio is about the same for 8 and 10 bit and for 4:2:0
  and 4:4:4. On macOS it is a little higher at 12/24 MP than at web sizes.
- **All threads:** 0.27–0.35 at 12/24 MP and 0.36–0.46 at web sizes on
  x86-64 (4 vCPUs); 0.55–0.66 on macOS (10 cores). These inputs are
  single-tile stills, and libaom gains little from threads on them. On the
  4-vCPU x86 hosts, libaom with 4 threads is slower than with one, both C and
  port (for example GH, 12 MP 8-bit 4:2:0: port 323 → 473 ms, C 277 → 448 ms),
  as in the 2026-11-17 record (column (c) 0.73). dav1d does thread on them.
- **Each port against its own original:** the libaom port takes 1.05–1.24×
  stock libaom (one thread), which matches the 2026-11-17 column (b)
  (1.12–1.33). The dav1d port takes 1.13–1.28× stock dav1d at the v1.0 SIMD
  scope on x86-64 and 1.16–1.33× on macOS. Unrestricted stock dav1d takes
  0.95–1.02× the time of the restricted build on these hosts.
- **The libaom port against stock dav1d (v1.0 scope):** 1.76–2.05× at one
  thread on macOS and 1.94–2.46× on x86-64. With threads it is 1.75–2.35×
  on macOS and 2.64–4.34× on x86-64.
- **The engine's own path** (`ferrisma-avif-read`, `port-aom-reader:t1`,
  8-bit only) times the same as `decode_codec aom 1` (ratio 0.98–1.00 on
  every record). The `port-aom` columns therefore stand for the engine.
- **Peak memory** (RSS of the whole process, decoded RGB included). One
  thread, 12 MP 8-bit 4:2:0: libaom port 251 MB, dav1d port 104–105 MB, C
  libaom 211–213 MB, C dav1d 65–66 MB on all hosts. 24 MP 10-bit 4:4:4:
  598–600, 307–308, 523–525 and 233 MB. With threads libaom grows about
  fourfold (port 12 MP: about 1,000 MB; 24 MP 10-bit 4:4:4: about 2,045 MB);
  dav1d grows by less than 10 %.

### Change since 2026-11-17

GH x86-64 gave the AVIF job the same CPU model both times (Intel Xeon
6975P-C). At 12 MP 8-bit 4:2:0 (the only input both records share), the
libaom port went from 322 to 323 ms and the dav1d port from 319 to 186 ms.
The ratio dav1d port / libaom port went from 0.99 to 0.58. In between came
the x86-64 AVX2 kernels for msac, loop filter, inverse transforms, CDEF and
intra prediction (libavif-rs ADR 0062, amendment log 2026-11-17 to
2026-11-20); on 11-17 the port ran its scalar translation on x86-64. For
macOS the two records cannot be compared: 11-17 measured a 3-vCPU M1 VM with
loads of up to 28.6, this record a local M1 Pro.

## Pixel identity

Decoded 8-bit RGB (the drivers' PPM output), SHA-256 per candidate, recorded
in `decoders-checks.json`:

- **x86-64 (GH and BS, both runs):** all 16 inputs identical across all 11
  candidates.
- **macOS ARM64 (both passes):** 11 of 16 inputs are identical across all
  candidates. On the other 5, all of them 10-bit (12 and 24 MP 4:2:0 and
  4:4:4, 1.4 MP 4:4:4), the two port decoders agree with each other and the
  two C decoders agree with each other, but the two groups differ. The
  difference is 2 to 163 samples per image, each off by 1.
- **Cause, checked:** the difference comes from C libavif's YUV-to-RGB
  conversion, not from an AV1 decoder. Apple clang contracts floating-point
  expressions by default (fused multiply-add); Rust does not. With C libavif
  rebuilt with `-ffp-contract=off` (same libaom and dav1d archives), C libaom,
  C dav1d, the libaom port and the dav1d port give identical RGB on **16 of
  16** inputs ([macos-fp-contract-check.txt](evidence-2026-11-22-decoders/macos-fp-contract-check.txt)).
  The 8-bit path does not use that float conversion, which is why the
  2026-11-17 record (8-bit inputs only) found identity everywhere.
- The two port decoders give identical RGB on every input on every host.

## Noise

- **macOS ARM64:** a shared machine with other agents running. The two
  machine-wide gate slots (`.orch/gate-lock.sh`) were both held for each
  pass, so no other gate ran, but other work did. 1-minute load averages
  during samples: pass 1 3.4–7.0, pass 2 2.0–3.3. The median spread
  (max − min) / median per cell is 4.1 % (pass 1) and 4.4 % (pass 2); 90 %
  of cells are within 9.4 % and 11.4 %. The largest spreads (up to 208 %)
  are single outlier samples with min ≈ median (see the full table). Between
  the two passes every ratio in the summary moves by at most 0.03, and most
  by 0.01 or less. The `tN` columns are the most exposed to the other load
  (10 threads on a machine with a load of 2–7).
- **x86-64 hosted:** 4 vCPUs, loads 1.0–2.3. Median spread 2.6–2.8 % (GH)
  and 4.5–4.7 % (BS). Between the two runs the summary ratios move by at
  most 0.03. GH ran an Intel Xeon 6975P-C both times and BS an AMD EPYC both
  times, so the x86 figures cover one Intel and one AMD part.

## Limits of this evidence

- **Single-tile inputs.** As in the 2026-11-13/17 records, the inputs are
  encoded on one thread, so each has one tile. Files written with tiles
  (libavif's autotiling when an encoder runs threaded, which the engine's
  own writer does for threads ≠ 1) would let libaom thread better. Not
  measured.
- **Photo content only.** One 12 MP and one 24 MP photograph, plus box-filtered
  1.4 MP and 0.34 MP versions of the 12 MP one, all at quality 60, speed 6.
  No alpha, no 12-bit, no lossless, no film grain, no image sequences.
- **Identity is checked on RGB8**, the engine's output. For 10-bit inputs
  that is weaker than comparing the decoded YUV. Bit identity of the YUV
  output is the job of the decoders' own gates (whole-image runs, libavif-rs
  ADR 0062).
- **x86-64 below AVX2** (SSSE3/SSE4.1-only CPUs) and AVX-512 hosts are not
  covered; both hosted CPUs select AVX2. Linux ARM64 has no AVIF port build
  (ADR-0026). No hosted macOS run (not requested).
- **No x86-64 instruction counts.** Callgrind under Colima was not run for
  this record: the hosted runs give real x86 timings, and the latest counts
  are in libavif-rs ADR 0062 (2026-11-20: +10.4 to +14.5 % instructions per
  decode over the old port). Those are instruction counts, not time.
- **Timer region:** decoder create + parse + decode + YUV→RGB8 (nearest
  upsampling). Process start, reading the file and writing the PPM fall
  outside. Peak RSS covers the whole process.

## Method

- **Tool:** [scripts/bench/ci-perf/decoders_avif.sh](../../scripts/bench/ci-perf/decoders_avif.sh).
  It builds everything with
  [codec_avif.sh](../../scripts/bench/ci-perf/codec_avif.sh)
  (`AVIF_BUILD_ONLY=1`), so each binary is built exactly as for the
  2026-11-17 record's AVIF job. That means libavif 1.4.2, libaom v3.15.2
  (release, D6 scope: no AVX-512, no NEON dotprod/i8mm/SVE) and dav1d 1.5.4
  (release, full assembly) from the tracked snapshots. The drivers are the
  C `decode_still.c` the script writes and the port examples
  `ferrisma-libavif` `decode_codec` and `ferrisma-avif-read` `decode_still`,
  built with the repository release profile.
- **Inputs:** `make_masters.sh` masters (photo12 4032×3024, photo24
  6000×4000). web14 (1344×1008) and web03 (672×504) are photo12
  box-filtered by 3 and 6. Each is encoded by the **original** (C
  `encode_still`, quality 60, speed 6, one thread) at 8 and 10 bit, 4:2:0
  and 4:4:4. Sizes and SHA-256 are in `decoders-inputs.json`.
- **Candidates, one interleaved plan per input:** `c-aom`, `c-dav1d-v1`
  (dav1d's assembly masked with `dav1d_set_cpu_flags_mask` to the v1.0
  scope: 0x2f on x86-64, NEON on AArch64), `c-dav1d` (unrestricted),
  `port-aom` and `port-dav1d` (`decode_codec`), each at one thread and at
  `maxThreads` = online CPUs (4 on the hosted runners, 10 on the Mac). Plus
  `port-aom-reader:t1`, the engine's safe reader, for 8-bit inputs.
- **Timing:** [run_interleaved.py](../../scripts/bench/ci-perf/run_interleaved.py):
  a fresh process per sample, candidate order alternating per repetition,
  one warm-up and 11 repetitions. The programs' own `elapsed_ms` timers,
  medians. Peak RSS comes from `/usr/bin/time` (`-v` on Linux, `-l` on macOS).
- **Machines:** local Apple M1 Pro (8 performance + 2 efficiency cores,
  32 GB), macOS 27.2, rustc 1.100.0-nightly (2026-09-06, the pinned
  toolchain), Apple clang. Hosted GitHub `ubuntu-24.04` (Intel Xeon 6975P-C,
  4 vCPUs) and Blacksmith `blacksmith-4vcpu-ubuntu-2404` (AMD EPYC,
  4 vCPUs), job `decoders` of
  [bench-v1-perf.yml](../../.github/workflows/bench-v1-perf.yml).
